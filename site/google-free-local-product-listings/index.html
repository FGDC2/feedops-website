Definition

What are Google Free Local Product Listings?

Google Free Local Product Listings allow retailers with physical stores to display products, pricing and live in-store availability across Google's shopping experiences for free. They are designed for nearby, in-store shopping intent. Unlike traditional Google Ads, there is no cost per click.

When a shopper searches for a product nearby, Google may display:

  • Product image and title
  • Price and store name
  • Distance from the shopper
  • Whether the item is in stock
  • Collection or pickup information, where available

Instead of only directing shoppers to buy online, Google can show them the exact product available in the nearest store. This helps shoppers make faster buying decisions and helps retailers convert local searches into store visits and in-store sales.

Google surfaces

Where do Free Local Product Listings appear?

Eligible local products can appear when people search close to a participating store, especially across Google Shopping and Google Maps. They may also appear across Google Search, Google Images and Google Lens.

As Google continues investing in AI-powered shopping experiences, accurate product and local inventory data becomes even more important. The same data sources help Google understand what your products are and where they are available.

Retail shopping has changed. Customers now search Google first. If Google does not know what is available in your store, your products cannot appear when nearby shoppers are looking for them.

Consumers increasingly expect to know whether a product is in stock, which store has it, what it costs and whether they can collect it today. Free Local Product Listings can answer those questions before the customer even visits your website.

Important distinction

Google Free Listings are for online sales. Free Local Listings are for nearby store inventory.

Google Shopping Free Listings promote products that shoppers can purchase online. They normally use your primary ecommerce product feed and send shoppers to a product page on your website to complete an online sale.

Google Free Local Product Listings promote products that are available inside a nearby physical store. They combine the primary product feed with store information and a local inventory feed so Google can show local price, availability, distance and pickup information when a shopper searches close by.

Implementation

What you need to get started.

Google requires several components to work together. The setup is manageable, but each layer needs consistent identifiers, pricing, store data and availability.

A verified Google Business Profile

Each physical store should have its own verified profile with accurate business details.

Google Merchant Center

Merchant Center manages your product information, destinations and local inventory.

An accurate primary product feed

Submit product titles, descriptions, images, prices, availability, brands and GTINs where available. Better data gives Google a clearer understanding of each product.

A local inventory feed

Tell Google which store stocks each product, along with store-level pricing, availability and collection information.

Linked store and Merchant Center data

Connect Merchant Center to the relevant Google Business Profiles so Google knows which locations belong to the account.

Validation

Check that product IDs, prices, inventory, store codes and policy requirements match across every system.

Ongoing feed-health monitoring

Inventory changes constantly. Monitor updates and Merchant Center diagnostics so local product data remains accurate.

Troubleshooting

Common problems retailers encounter.

Most local listing problems are straightforward to fix once they are identified. Common issues include:

  • Incorrect product identifiers or product IDs that do not match
  • Incorrect store codes
  • Inventory that does not update frequently enough
  • Merchant Center errors or missing GTINs
  • Pricing differences between the website and store
  • Google Business Profiles that are not linked correctly

Trace a small sample of products from the primary feed through the local inventory feed, store mapping and live shopper experience. Clean examples often reveal the wider pattern quickly.

Launch timing

How to launch before your next sales period.

Do not wait until a peak sales period to begin. Google needs time to validate feeds, process inventory, approve stores and build confidence in your product data. Starting several weeks early gives you time to resolve issues and establish reliable listings before demand increases.

Start before peak demand

Allow several weeks for setup, review and corrections instead of treating the launch as a last-minute campaign task.

Connect your store locations

Verify each Google Business Profile and link the relevant store locations to Google Merchant Center.

Prepare and match product data

Make product IDs, prices, availability and store codes consistent across the primary feed and local inventory feed.

Submit and resolve diagnostics

Submit the feeds, review Merchant Center diagnostics and correct product, inventory, policy and store-mapping issues.

Validate the shopper experience

Check a representative sample of products and stores to confirm that local availability, price and store details are accurate.

Monitor and maintain the launch

Keep inventory updates frequent and monitor diagnostics so listings remain accurate as demand changes.

Frequently asked questions

Google Free Local Product Listings FAQs.

Are Google Free Local Product Listings really free?

Yes. There is no advertising cost to appear in Google Free Local Product Listings. You do not pay per click or per impression.

Do I need Google Merchant Center?

Yes. Merchant Center is required to submit and manage your product data and local inventory.

Do I need a local inventory feed?

Yes. Your primary product feed tells Google what products you sell, while your local inventory feed tells Google which products are available in each physical store.

Where can Google Free Local Product Listings appear?

Eligible listings can appear across Google surfaces where nearby shoppers research products and stores, including Google Search, Google Shopping and Google Maps.

Which retailers are eligible for Free Local Product Listings?

They are designed for retailers with physical locations where customers can buy or collect products. Your business needs accurate product, store and local inventory data and must meet Google Merchant Center policies.

Can a retailer with one store use Free Local Product Listings?

Yes. The program can support a single physical store or a large multi-location network. Each participating location needs accurate store details and inventory mapping.

How long does it take to launch Free Local Product Listings?

Timing depends on feed quality, store verification and the number of issues found during review. Allow several weeks for setup, Google processing, validation and corrections before an important sales period.

How often should local inventory data be updated?

Update local inventory as frequently as stock and prices change. Frequent automated updates reduce the risk of showing products that are unavailable or displaying outdated store-level information.

What is the difference between Google Shopping Free Listings and Free Local Product Listings?

Google Shopping Free Listings primarily promote products available for online purchase. Free Local Product Listings show products available at nearby physical stores and rely on store-level inventory data.

What is the difference between Free Local Product Listings and Local Inventory Ads?

Free Local Product Listings provide organic local visibility at no advertising cost. Local Inventory Ads are paid advertisements that use the same product, store and local inventory data and can receive sponsored placement.

Can I use Free Local Product Listings and Local Inventory Ads together?

Yes. You can use free local visibility alongside paid Local Inventory Ads. Both use the same core product, store and local inventory foundation, while the ads add budget, bidding and sponsored reach.

Can I use Shopify, BigCommerce, Magento or WooCommerce?

Yes. Most major ecommerce platforms can provide the product data required, although the setup varies depending on your platform and how your inventory is managed.
