Google Local Inventory questions

Google Local Inventory feeds: frequently asked questions

What are Google Local Inventory Ads?

Local Inventory Ads show nearby shoppers products available at physical retail locations. Depending on the format, an ad may include product details, store availability, local price, distance and pickup information.

Do we need a Google Merchant Center account?

Yes. The central product catalogue and local inventory data are managed through Google Merchant Center. Google Ads, Merchant Center and store locations must be connected correctly for paid campaigns.

Do we need Google Business Profile?

Participating stores generally need Google Business Profile or another applicable Google-supported location setup. Feed store codes must match connected store-location records.

Can we use our existing Google Shopping feed?

Yes. Your existing product data can normally supply the central catalogue. The local implementation adds store-specific inventory and connects each record with the relevant product and store.

Can we activate only selected stores or products?

Yes. FeedOps can control participation by store, region, category, brand, product, stock, price, margin, promotion or other agreed rules.

Can we use Local Inventory Ads with Performance Max?

Yes. Eligible local inventory can be used in Performance Max campaigns. The structure depends on whether campaigns combine online and local objectives or focus on store goals.

Can we use free local listings without paid advertising?

Eligible retailers can activate free local listings, paid Local Inventory Ads or both. Your business controls which programs are activated.

Can Local Inventory Ads support click and collect?

Eligible pickup experiences can include same-day, next-day and later pickup. Your source data, website, checkout and operations must support the promise shown.

How frequently will inventory be updated?

Frequency depends on source systems, inventory volatility, connection method, catalogue size and Google’s supported processing methods. We do not describe a setup as real-time unless the complete data path supports it.

Can stores have different prices?

Store-specific pricing can be supported when available from the source system and implemented consistently across the feed and customer experience.

Who manages the advertising campaigns?

Your internal media team or agency manages campaigns, budgets, bidding and targets. FeedOps manages the product and inventory data and supports the campaign team when feed changes affect advertising.

What happens after launch?

FeedOps monitors feed delivery, store and product matching, record errors, Merchant Center warnings and eligibility. We maintain rules and coordinate issues originating in inventory sources, websites, store data or advertising accounts.
