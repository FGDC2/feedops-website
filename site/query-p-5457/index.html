What Are Google Shopping Free Listings?

In short, Google Shopping free listings are unpaid organic product listings that connect consumers to your online shop. They appear as product images with details like price and availability, linking directly to your store.

These items show up in the Google Shopping tab, Google Search results, and across local listings in Google Maps, helping both online and local customers find your products quickly.

Your free listings are powered by your Google Merchant Account. When your product data — title, description, image, price, and product availability — meets shopping ads policies, Google can automatically show your products in both global and local product listings programs.

Think of this as rich product snippets for ecommerce. Instead of optimizing web pages, you optimize your product information so Google understands your catalog and presents your products to the right shoppers.

How to Set Up Product Data in Google Shopping Free Listings In Google Merchant Center

Setting up Google free listings is straightforward and can be done in less than an hour. Here is you checklist.

Step-by-Step Checklist

  • 📧 Step 1: Create a Google Merchant Center Account – Sign in with your Google Account and visit merchants.google.com to create your Google Merchant Center profile.

  • ✅ Step 2: Verify and Claim Your Website Store – Confirm your website ownership using Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics, or a meta tag.

  •  📤 Step 3: Upload Your Product Feed –  Upload your feed via a Google Sheet, file, or by connecting your ecommerce platform such as Shopify or WooCommerce.

  • 🌐 Step 4: Choose Country and Language –  Make sure your feed matches your market’s currency and language.

  • 🌟 Step 5: Enable the Free Listings Program  – In Google Merchant Centre, open Growth → Manage Programs → Free Listings, then click Get Started.

  • 🛠️  Step 6: Fix Merchant Centre Feed Errors – Before showing your products, Google checks for issues such as:

    • 🏷️ Missing identifiers (GTIN, Brand, MPN) 

    • 📉 Price or stock mismatches 

    • 📸 Missing or poor-quality images 

Once approved, 🚀 your products appear across Google’s free surfaces, including local listings if linked to your Google Business Profile.

How to Measure Success in Google Analytics (Organic Google Shopping)

Once your listings are live, track google shopping results.

Option 1: Merchant Center

Go to Performance → Dashboard → Free Listings. Review impressions, clicks, and top-performing products.

Option 2: Google Analytics 4 (GA4)

Free listings traffic appears as Organic Google Shopping.
To track:

  1. Open Reports → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition.

  2. Filter by Source / Medium = google / organic.

  3. Add Session source platform to locate “Organic Google Shopping.”

This shows conversions, revenue, and engagement from free listings — separate from regular organic search.

Pro Tip: Create a segment for “Organic Google Shopping” in GA4 to measure long-term performance and refine your strategy.

FAQs & Google Shopping Free Listings

1. Is Google Shopping free to use?

Yes. Google Shopping now includes a free listings program that lets you show your products in the Google Shopping tab, Google Search results, and other Google surfaces — all without paying per click. You just need a Google Merchant Account and a verified website.

2. What are Google Shopping free listings?

They are organic product listings that display your products on Google. These listings look like Shopping Ads but don’t cost anything. Google pulls your product data from the Merchant Center and matches it to shoppers searching for similar items.

4. Do I need Google Ads to use free listings?

No. You don’t need to run Google Ads to appear in free listings. However, many online retailers combine both free and paid listings to increase visibility and test performance before scaling with paid ads.

5. What’s the difference between paid Shopping Ads and free listings?

Paid ads appear across more places — like YouTube, Display, and Gmail — while free listings appear mainly in the Shopping tab and Search results. Paid ads use bids; free listings rely on feed quality and relevance. Both must follow Google’s shopping ads policies.

6. How do I qualify for Google Shopping free listings?

You’ll need a verified Google Merchant Account, a working product feed, and a site that meets Google’s policies. Your feed must include all required product data attributes and accurate information about price and product availability.

7. Can local stores appear in Google Shopping free listings?

Yes. If you connect your Google Business Profile to your Merchant Center, your store can show in local listings through the local product listings program. This helps nearby shoppers find your products and visit your store for a purchase.

8. Where will my free listings appear?

Your products can show up in:

  • The Google Shopping tab

  • Google Search results

  • Google Images

  • The shopping knowledge panel

  • Google Maps (for local feeds)

Google decides placement based on product quality and search relevance.

9. What information should my product feed include?

Your feed must contain complete, accurate, and up-to-date product information so your listings meet Google’s data standards. Include the following key product data attributes:

  • id – A unique product ID.

  • title – The product name (include key details like color, size, and type).

  • description – A short, clear explanation of the product’s features and benefits.

  • link – The landing page URL on your website.

  • image_link – A high-quality image showing the product clearly.

  • availability – Current product availability (e.g. in stock, out of stock, preorder).

  • price – The product’s current price in the correct currency.

  • brand – The brand name.

  • gtin – Global Trade Item Number (UPC/EAN/ISBN, if applicable).

  • mpn – Manufacturer Part Number (if no GTIN).

  • condition – Whether it’s new, used, or refurbished.

  • shipping – Cost and country for delivery.

  • google_product_category – The category that matches Google’s taxonomy.

  • product_type – Your internal product category (helps for filtering).

  • item_group_id – To group product variants like color or size.

  • sale_price and sale_price_effective_date – If you’re running a sale.

  • identifier_exists – Set to true or false depending on identifiers provided.

  • additional_image_link – Optional extra image URLs.

Adding these fields helps Google show your products more often in organic product listings and local listings, improving visibility and clicks.

10. How do I track performance for free listings?

You can track performance in:

  • Google Merchant Center → Performance → Free Listings for clicks and impressions.

  • Google Analytics 4 (GA4) → filter for Organic Google Shopping to see sessions, conversions, and revenue.

 

11. What are the benefits of using Google Shopping free listings?

They give you free visibility, attract organic traffic, improve brand discovery, and strengthen your rich product snippets. They also help you identify top-performing products for future Google Ads campaigns.

12. Can I use free listings for international sales?

Yes. You can set your country of sale and language in your Merchant Centre. Make sure to display prices in the correct currency and comply with local policies and tax requirements.

13. Do free listings show all products?

Not always. Google shows only approved products that meet its shopping ads policies. Missing GTINs, incorrect pricing, or incomplete product information can prevent items from appearing.

14. Can I use free and paid listings together?

Absolutely. Many successful online stores use both. Free listings boost organic reach, while paid ads drive conversions. The data you gather from free listings helps you optimize your paid strategy.

15. What should I do if my listings are disapproved?

Check your Merchant Center Diagnostics section. Google flags issues like policy violations or missing data. Visit Google Merchant Center Help to understand and fix the problem before re-uploading your feed.

19. Can I manage multiple stores or channels under one account?
